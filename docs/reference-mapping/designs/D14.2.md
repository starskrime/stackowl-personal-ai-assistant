## Why this exists

The Ask: *"Which of their 84 name a capability we lack? (Walk the list — it doubles as a
feature audit.)"* A count of 84 against 34 reads like a 50-command hole. It is not, and the
only way to know that is to walk it.

**Measured 2026-09-06: 84 theirs, 34 ours, 9 names in common, 75 theirs-only. Of those 75,
SIX name a capability we genuinely lack, and one of the six is already a mapped item.** The
rest are the same capabilities under different names, terminal-display toggles, or bound to
their hosted product.

### Terminal display and vendor product (25) — not capabilities

`/battery`, `/footer`, `/indicator`, `/redraw`, `/skin`, `/statusbar`, `/timestamps`,
`/verbose`, `/busy`, `/clear`, `/copy`, `/paste`, `/image`, `/prompt`, `/title` are TUI

`/egress`, `/fast`, `/profile` are tied to their hosted product, its billing, or its
runtime. Counting these as a capability gap would inflate the hole by two-thirds.

`/blueprint` ("set up an automation from a blueprint template") is deliberately excluded
from that list. It is absent, and it **contradicts a standing decision**: this assistant
self-extends — it designs and registers its own tools and skills rather than instantiating
hand-written templates. A blueprint catalogue would be the hand-built path the rule refuses.

`/hatch` and `/pet` generate and adopt mascot characters. Not capabilities.

## Invariants

1. **A count is not a gap.** 84 − 34 = 50 suggests fifty missing features; the walk finds
   six, one of which is already mapped. Any future "we have N, they have M" comparison in
   this programme should be walked before it is believed.
2. **A capability is located by FILE, not by name.** Every "we have this" row above names
   the module that provides it; the two rows that first looked present —
   `profile_backup.py` for `/profile` and `todo.py` for `/suggestions` — turned out to be
   browser-profile tarballs and a did-you-mean hint respectively.
