## Why this exists

The map said StackOwl had a tool-exposure problem: *"Both register 75 tools. The
difference is entirely in exposure discipline: the reference ships ~40 by default and
gates the rest; StackOwl presents up to 150."* A brainstorm on 2026-08-04 found that
sentence wrong on both sides and produced a replacement — *"the gap is INVERTED: their
default is 54, our live average is 4"* — then stopped, because every one of its 64
measured turns was a background lane. Bakir refused to close the item on background data
and it sat `architect: blocked` on **interactive traffic** for eighteen days.

That traffic now exists. Measuring it showed the count is at **parity** — and that both
earlier figures were wrong in the same way, because both compared a **cap** against a
**floor**. The item's product is the correction, plus three defects that only appeared
because someone finally opened this path and counted.

## Model

Three numbers govern how many tools reach the model, and until this change they did not
all come from the same place.

| Name | Value | Defined at | Applies to | Operator-settable |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `orchestrator.tool_count_cap` | 40 | `config/settings.py:399` | the budgeted path, **and now both `select()` paths** | yes, `hot_reload: True` |
| `PresentationConfig.cap` | `_DEFAULT_CAP` = 36 | `tools/_infra/presentation.py:63`, `:142` | `select()` — profile, pins, hydrated, and planned envelopes | **now, via `max_tools`** |
| `HARD_TOOL_COUNT_CAP` | 150 | `pipeline/context_budget.py:20` | fallback when nothing is configured | no |

`HARD_TOOL_COUNT_CAP` is **not a ceiling.** `resolve_tool_count_cap` (`context_budget.py:23`)
returns the configured value whenever it is ≥ 1; 150 is only the fallback. A
`tool_count_cap` of 5000 would be honoured. The map read 150 as our default exposure and
compared it against the reference's declared set — it exceeds the entire live catalog (79)
by 71 and has never been reached. The measured maximum ever presented is **40**.

### The catalog

| | count | source |
|---|---|---|
| registered in the tree | 77 | `ToolRegistry.with_defaults()`, driven in-process |
| live, including agent-authored | 79 | + 2 from `~/.stackowl/workspace/tools/learned/*.json` via `tools/meta/learned_tool_loader.py:56` |
| guaranteed floor (non-evictable) | 18 | `_DEFAULT_BASE ∪ _DEFAULT_ALWAYS`, `presentation.py:74`, `:120` |

79 is the number that is actually capped. It reconciles with the logs by arithmetic:
`presented:30 + dropped_count:49 = 79`, and `tool_search`'s `catalog_size: 78` is 79 minus
itself.

## Lifecycle

`to_provider_schema` picks exactly one of four paths, **in this order**. Ordering is
behaviour: the first match wins and the later branches never run.

```
to_provider_schema(protocol, profile, pins, hydrated, restrict_to, budget, max_tools)
  │
  ├─1─ restrict_to is not None ────────► select(restrict_to=…)     "planned envelope"
  │      always_present ∪ (planned ∩ catalog), cut at cap
  │      NOT memoized — an envelope is per-task by construction
  │
  ├─2─ budget is not None ─────────────► rank_candidates + fit_items   "budgeted"
  │      guaranteed ∪ discretionary ranked by (-usage_score, -priority, name)
  │      cut at min(cap, what fits in tokens).  MEMOIZED per lane.
  │
  ├─3─ profile/pins/hydrated all None ─► every registered tool
  │
  └─4─ otherwise ──────────────────────► select(…)                  "profile"
         guaranteed ∪ pins ∪ hydrated ∪ group tools, cut at cap
```

Path 1 is checked **before** path 2, so a task with a planned envelope never reaches the
budgeter — which is why it also never reached the budgeter's log line.

## Invariants

> **I1.** The cap that a turn is cut at is the operator's configured cap on **every**
> path, not only the budgeted one. A caller that supplies no cap keeps the previous
> default exactly; nothing widens silently.
>
> **I2.** A cut in the presented set always leaves a record naming **which** tools were
> cut. No path may truncate and return without a witness.
>
> **I3.** The `dropped` field names **every** dropped tool. It is never a prefix of the
> answer, and `dropped_count` always equals `len(dropped)`.
>
> **I4.** The discovery pair (`tool_search`, `tool_describe`) is non-evictable on every
> path, however tight the cap or the envelope. An owl must always be able to look for
> what it lacks.

Each maps to a test — see [Verification](#verification).

## What was measured, and what it corrected

Window: `~/.stackowl/logs/stackowl-2026-08-{15..21}.jsonl` plus the live `stackowl.jsonl`,
read 2026-08-22. Source records are `[pipeline] execute: context budget` (INFO), which
already carried `tools_count`; the 2026-08-04 note was right that no new instrument was
needed and the blocker was traffic.

### The interactive path, finally

Interactive records were split by trace shape, because a `retry-*` / `*-fix` trace on an
interactive lane is a self-heal retry of that conversation and not a fresh turn:

| population | n | avg | median | p90 | min | max |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| interactive **live** | 159 | 21.9 | **30** | 36 | 3 | 40 |
| interactive **retry** | 148 | 23.9 | 26 | 36 | 4 | 40 |
| background (goal, incident, recovery) | 1,108 | 28.2 | 30 | — | — | 40 |

The "avg 4.0, max 24" that raised the whole under-presentation question was goal lanes
only. On the interactive path the median is **30**.

### Both prior figures compared a cap against a floor

The reference platform's `_HERMES_CORE_TOOLS` is **54 names — and 21 of them are env-gated
by `check_fn`**, absent unless the operator has set something: 4 desktop-GUI tools
(`HERMES_DESKTOP`), 4 Home Assistant (`HASS_TOKEN`), 12 kanban (`HERMES_KANBAN_TASK` or an
explicit profile), and 1 computer-use (a driver install). **A plain session there presents
33.**

Ours: median 30, p90 36, max 40. **That is parity**, and it is the first time this item
has been measured against the right number.

### The mechanisms differ even though the outcomes match

The reference has **no tool-count cap anywhere**. Its 54 is a never-defer *floor* — its own
header says *"Always-load means always-load. No exceptions."* — and its only gate is
window-relative: defer when the deferrable schemas reach `threshold_pct` of context
(default 10%), or a flat 20,000 tokens when the window is unknown
(`tools/tool_search.py:234-258` in the read-only clone).

Our entire 77-tool catalog is **19,310 tokens = 7.37%** of a 262,144 window (driven
in-process through the real registry, 2026-08-22), and the presented set at cap 40 costs
8,495 tokens synthetically / **9,369 tokens = 3.57%** at its largest in live traffic.
**Their gate would never fire here.**
So the two platforms reach the same presented count by opposite means: they would present
everything and never reach their threshold; we cut at a count that binds on **100%** of the
turns where anything is dropped (`limited_by` = `tool_count_cap` on 265 of 265 populated
records, `token_budget` on **zero**).

Same answer today; different exposure to tomorrow. That difference is `ESC-35`, not this
document's to settle.

### Does the cap cost anything? The harm test says no

The falsifiable question is not "how many were dropped" but "did a turn ever want one".

- **Not one turn in eight days called a tool that existed in the catalog and had been
  dropped by the cap.** The 5 unknown-tool events name `memory_search`, `scout`, `gmail`,
  `gmail_assist`, `tool_call` — none of which is registered. `gmail` is a real capability
  gap and no cap change would surface it.
- Bucketed by presented count, the turns where the cap **binds** are the healthiest in the
  window: 12 interactive turns at exactly 40 produced 0 `tool_search`, 0 `tool_describe`,
  0 bounds refusals, 0 guardrail warnings. Every unit of damage sits in the ≤10 bucket —
  the planned-envelope path, where the cap is not the constraint.

So the cap's **value** does not move in this item. What does move is that it now applies
where the operator was told it applies, and that a cut is no longer invisible.

## The three defects this shipped

### A — the operator's cap reached only one of three paths

`PresentationConfig.cap` defaults to `_DEFAULT_CAP = 36`, and all three `ToolPresentation()`
construction sites in `registry.py` passed **no config**. So `select()` was pinned to 36
while `tool_count_cap` reached only the budgeter. Two copies of one rule, with one of them
wired.

Measured live: **80 turns presented exactly 36 tools** while the configured cap was 30 and
then 40 — `always_present` is 4 and `36 − 4 = 32` planned, the branch saturating. The
settings docstring tells the operator to *"lower it (e.g. 12-18) for weak/quantized
models"*; on every turn through this branch that instruction was false.

> **Correction, and it matters.** An earlier draft of this document attributed those 80
> turns to the **planner's** task envelope. That is wrong and the planner could never have
> caused it: across all eight retained logs there are 104 `[planner] plan: envelope set`
> events and their sizes are 3 (×51), 4 (×29), 5 (×19), 6 (×3), 7 (×2) — **the largest plan
> ever made is seven tools**, which presents 11, not 36.
>
> The real trigger is the **self-heal ban path**. At `pipeline/steps/execute.py:1345-1350`,
> when `state.banned_capabilities` is non-empty, `_restrict_to_for_turn` builds
> `all_names - banned` and assigns it to `restrict_to` — deliberately, because its docstring
> says a restriction *"rides the existing non-memoized `restrict_to` branch"*. That set is
> **75–78 names**, and it then meets a branch built for a small plan. Correlation is total:
> 81 traces carry the `banned capabilities excluded` line, its own `presented` field reads
> 75–78 going in, and **all 71** of the 36-turns that can be joined to a trace carry it.
> Turns with no ban never produce 36.
>
> This makes the fix matter **more**, not less. The silent cut was not a latent edge case —
> it fired on every self-heal retry that had banned a capability, roughly ten a day, and
> each lost about **forty-one tools with no log line at any level**. A retry is the moment
> the platform most needs the right toolset, and it was the moment it silently received the
> alphabetically-first 36.
>
> How the error happened, because the shape repeats: `restrict_to` has **two producers**,
> and I checked only the one the parameter is named after. The log line I reached for
> (`plan: envelope set`) belongs to the innocent producer. Grep every assignment to the
> variable, not the feature it is named after.

**Fixed** by a `max_tools` parameter on `to_provider_schema` that builds the
`PresentationConfig` for both `select()` paths, and by `execute.py` passing `_max_tools`
on the envelope call site as it already did on the budgeted one. Omitting it keeps
`_DEFAULT_CAP` exactly, so no existing caller changes behaviour.

### B — the envelope path truncated with no log line at all

`presentation.py`'s `restrict_to` branch computed `planned[:budget]` and returned **above
both** the entry log and the drop log. `[presentation] select: eligible tools NOT presented`
fired **zero times in eight days** against 955 from `to_provider_schema`. A task envelope
wider than the cap lost its tail with nothing written at any level.

**Fixed** by emitting the same INFO record from that branch, carrying `planned` alongside
`dropped`/`presented`/`cap` so a reader can tell "the plan was too big" from "the cap was
too small".

### C — the eviction evidence was blind to half the catalog

Both drop lines emitted `dropped[:20]`. That slice was not arbitrary: `dropped` follows
rank order, and rank order for a tool with neither a usage score nor a declared priority is
the **alphabet**. So the field had a fixed alphabetical ceiling —`objective`, `run_tests`,
`send_message`, `session_search`, `shell`, `todo`, `update_plan` and `web_search` could
never appear in it however often they were dropped. `dropped_truncated` was `true` on
**178 of 178** records in a day: never once false, so it marked the permanent state rather
than an edge case.

**Fixed** by emitting the whole list. It is bounded by construction — at most the catalog,
of short strings — which is the reasoning `cache_audit` already applies to its own name
lists. `dropped_truncated` stays in the record as a permanently-`false` contract marker so
a consumer does not have to guess which era wrote the line.

## What was deliberately NOT built

**The cap was not raised, and no new selection mechanism was added.** The harm test came
back clean, `limited_by` names the token budget zero times in 265 records, and D05.2
already records what a per-turn recompute here costs the prompt cache. A mechanism for a
constraint that does not bind is surface area.

**The ordering was not changed.** `presentation_priority` is declared on **8 of 77 tools
and all 8 are browser tools** (`browser_snapshot` 100, `browser_type`/`navigate`/`click` 90,
`wait_for`/`scroll`/`extract` 70, `screenshot` 60); the other 69 are 0. The rank key is
`(-usage_score, -declared_priority, name)`, so for 69 of 77 it collapses to the alphabet.
Reproduced with the real registry at cold start, cap 40: the presented set is 18 guaranteed
+ 22 discretionary, **20 of the 22 are `browser_*`**, and the 37 dropped include `clarify`,
`send_message`, `delegate_task`, `web_search`, `search_files`, `objective` and
`execute_code`. `ESC-9`'s own comment says the alphabet collapse *"is how a browser owl lost
the tools that let it see and type"* — it was fixed for the browser family only.

That is real and it is **`ESC-36`**, because choosing which tools deserve priority changes
what the model sees on every cold-start turn. It is product intent, not a defect with an
obvious repair.

## Failure modes

| Failure | Detected by | Recovery | User sees |
|---|---|---|---|
| The cap cuts a tool the turn needed | **nothing today** — the presented membership is not recorded per turn, so a registered-but-evicted tool that is never called is indistinguishable from one nobody wanted | overflow stays reachable via `tool_search` (794 calls in the window), at one round-trip | nothing; the model routes through discovery |
| The planned envelope is wider than the cap | the new INFO line, with `planned` beside `dropped` | none — the tail is cut | nothing |
| Guaranteed floor ≥ the configured cap | the WARNING at `registry.py` (has never fired) | none — every discretionary tool is starved | an owl that silently cannot do anything outside its base set |
| A broken manifest during ranking | `_declared_priority` returns 0 | tool keeps its place in the by-name tail | nothing |
| Config sets a cap below the floor | the same WARNING | none | as above |

The first row is the honest gap and is stated rather than glossed: this document can say
what was cut, and cannot say whether it mattered.

## Standing watch

`I1` and `I2` are proven by test and **not** observed in production. Both need a
`restrict_to` set **wider than the cap** — and the trigger to watch is **not** the planner,
whose largest plan in eight days was 7 tools. It is
`[pipeline] execute: banned capabilities excluded from the presented set`, the self-heal
ban path, which produces a `restrict_to` of 75–78 names. **Zero** such turns have run since
this boot, so both invariants have had no production opportunity at all.

That is a better statement of the wait than "needs traffic", and it is the same shape
`D05.4` ended in. `D05.4`'s resolution was Bakir's call — close on the integration-test
evidence, keep the watch — and it is **not** applied here by analogy: this item's central
claim (`I3`) closed on live production data with a 179-of-179 versus 16-of-16 contrast, so
the item's substance is validated and only the two smaller invariants are held.

### Where we diverged from the reference platform, and why

**We keep a count cap; they have none.** Their bound is purely window-relative and would
never activate on this hardware (7.37% of catalog against a 10% gate). We keep a count for a
reason their deployment does not have: this platform's only enabled backend is a single
weak/quantized local model, and the cap's own justification is a claim about the model
derailing, not about tokens running out. On a 262k window the token budget is unreachable
by a factor of ~15 — for it to bind, the fixed cost would have to exceed 242,834 tokens,
and the largest ever measured is 15,734.

**We also diverge on where the floor sits.** Their never-defer core is 54 declared / 33
effective and is a *floor* — everything above it still ships until the gate opens. Ours is
18 guaranteed, and the cap is a *ceiling* above it. The consequence is that their model
sees strictly more as their catalog grows and ours sees a constant 40. That asymmetry is
comfortable today and is exactly what `ESC-35` exists to decide, so it is named here rather
than left for a future reader to discover and "fix" back.

**We are at or ahead on every non-count idea they have.** Progressive disclosure exists
here and is load-bearing (`tool_search` is the single most-dispatched tool in the window,
794 calls). Dangling cross-reference stripping is `D05.6`
(`tools/_infra/cross_refs.py::strip_dangling_references`). And where they answer an unknown
tool name by printing the catalog — which primes the model to hallucinate more names — we
return a recovery action instead (`pipeline/steps/execute.py`, "build it with `tool_build`").
