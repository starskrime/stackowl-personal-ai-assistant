## Why this exists

**Not for the reason the map gave.** D10.6 was rated `AHEAD` because
`skills/skill_relevance.py` scored skills per turn and the reference platform did not.
ESC-10 **deleted that file on 2026-08-15**. We removed the scoring and kept the cap, so the
platform now does something *strictly worse* than the reference: it presents an alphabetical
tail-break of 16 skills out of 180. Verdict corrected at open to `PARTIAL / P1 / close_gap`.

The measured situation:

| fact | value |
|---|---|
| skills in the catalogue | **179** (14 builtin, 165 agent-authored) |
| presented per turn | **16** (9 SUMMARY + 7 bare names) |
| dropped per turn | **163**, on 95.5% of assemblies (2,905 truncation WARNINGs) |
| never loaded in 8 days | **171 of 180** |
| distinct skills ever loaded | **8**; two account for 400 of 427 loads |
| corpus authorship | **131 creates, 100% agent, 0 human**, growing ~1.5/day |

**The operator-facing framing, which is the only one that earns the item's priority:** this
corpus is not a library anyone browses — it is *the platform's memory of what it learned*.
It writes ~1.5 lessons a day and reads eight a week. The question is not "can a skill be
found" but **"does the platform apply what it already learned, or re-derive it?"**

## What the panel rejected, and why it matters

The obvious move was to merge near-duplicate skills — the evidence-brief family occupies
**5 of the 9 SUMMARY slots, 56.6% of the presented budget**. **Both lenses rejected it, for
independent reasons**, and that agreement is stronger than either alone.

**The bodies are not duplicates.** Seven skills, **nine distinct section names**, **three
incompatible missing-data conventions** (`"Not provided"` / `UNKNOWN` / a Gaps section), two
header levels, and two mandate `memory` calls that a third explicitly forbids. One states the
contract outright: *"merging sections breaks downstream parser expectations."* The duplication
is real in the 60-char `description` (two ARE byte-identical) and false in the 3,300–3,900-char
body — because `_resolve_text` renders `description — when_to_use`, not the body.

> Merging would destroy seven procedures to reclaim ~2,163 chars of duplicated **summary**.
> That is fixing the catalogue by deleting the corpus, and the catalogue is not made of the
> corpus.

**A clusterer would have caused real harm.** `rca_evidence_verifier` and
`rca-verifier-evidence-brief` share every token with the gatherers and are the **inverse
role** — *"Do not use it for collecting evidence."* Any name- or description-similarity
clustering sweeps them in and silently destroys the RCA pipeline's separation of duties.
**Mandatory negative test for anything that ever clusters skills.**

**And the last such pass left wreckage nobody saw.** `skill_ownership` holds 111 rows and
**20 point at skills that no longer exist** — 14 on the live `rca_gatherer` owl — every one a
`-N` name from a previous collapse. There is no foreign key (migration 0107 says so),
consolidation writes no audit row, and `store.delete()` never calls the
`purge_skill_ownership` that exists for it. Invisible because `hydrate_skill_ownership`
reports `attached: 10` against 111 rows and the gap lives in three `log.startup.debug` calls.

## The premise was a constant

`_DEFAULT_CAP = 4000` (`instruction_injector.py:13`) has **two references, both mechanical,
and no measurement behind it.** The reference platform has **no cap at all** — it renders all
69 skills at ~85 chars each and its code states the rule:

> *"NEVER remove entries entirely: agent-created skills are the model's project memory, and
> models don't reach for `skills_list` to rediscover what the index stops showing them."*

**Measured cost of the whole corpus**, against a 262,144-token window whose largest call ever
was 62,724 (23.9%):

| render | presented in a 4,000 cap | all 179 |
|---|---|---|
| current (per-entry fence + `(skill_view X)` suffix + full `when_to_use`) | **9** SUMMARY | 77,031 chars |
| **lean fence, suffix dropped, `when_to_use` intact** | **12** | 57,895 |
| lean + `when_to_use` capped 120 | 17 | 37,860 |
| lean + `when_to_use` capped 60 | 24 | 27,312 |
| lean + `when_to_use` dropped | 37 | 16,224 |

**The cost driver is `when_to_use` (median 234 chars), not the fence (median description 52).**
A correction to my own earlier claim: I attributed the saving to "a leaner render". The fence
and suffix are worth ~26%; the rest is `when_to_use`.

## The split: lossless now, lossy escalated

**Stage 1 — LOSSLESS. Ladder rung 1, shipping now.**

1. **Drop the per-entry `(skill_view <name>)` suffix.** It is *already stated in the section
   header* — `_AVAILABLE_HEADER` reads "call skill_view <name> to load before using". The
   per-entry repetition is redundant with its own heading, 14+`len(name)` chars × every entry.
2. **Collapse the per-entry fence to one block-level fence.** Each untrusted entry currently
   carries `<skill_reference name="…" source="…" trust="untrusted">…</skill_reference>` —
   ~70 chars of repeated attributes per entry.

**Zero information is lost.** Every skill still carries its full `description — when_to_use`.
Presented goes **9 → 12** (+33%) inside the unchanged cap.

**Stage 1b — LOSSY, ESCALATED (ESC-63).** Capping `when_to_use` and/or raising the cap to
present the entire corpus. This trades **breadth for depth**: more skills visible, each with
less about *when* to reach for it. Since `when_to_use` is precisely the "when" signal, and the
acceptance metric is *load rate*, cutting it could plausibly **reduce** loads while increasing
visibility. Not a judgement to make blind.

## Why the block fence is safe

`prompt_safety.neutralize` **strips `<` and `>` outright** — its docstring: *"prevents closing
or forging XML-style fence tags"* — and also strips `"`, drops line-start headers, and
collapses all whitespace to single spaces.

Two consequences that carry the security property:

> **I1.** A skill body cannot break out of any fence, per-entry or block-level, because the
> characters needed to close one do not survive neutralization.
> **I2.** A neutralized entry **cannot create a newline**, so one-line-per-entry is
> structurally enforced and entries cannot forge each other's delimiters.

So the per-entry wrapper contributes *delimitation*, not *defence*. The defence is
`neutralize`, applied per entry, and it is **unchanged** — including
`_PER_SKILL_NEUTRALIZE_CAP`, which is deliberately not touched.

## Invariants

> **I1.** No skill body can close or forge a fence tag. — *`prompt_safety` strips `<`/`>`.*
> **I2.** Each catalogue entry occupies exactly one line. — *whitespace collapse.*
> **I3.** Every entry is individually neutralized; the block fence adds to that, never replaces it.
> **I4.** No skill loses information in Stage 1 — `description` and `when_to_use` both survive intact.
> **I5.** The rendered block is byte-identical across turns of one session, so D01.1's frozen
> prompt is unaffected. — *the catalogue is a function of the corpus, not the query.*
> **I6.** Builtin (trusted) rendering is unchanged.

## Laws

**Law 1 (caching)** — untouched. The catalogue is already query-independent and frozen per
session; this changes its *shape*, not its *stability*. `skills_len` will shift once, at
deploy, then hold.

**Law 2 (narrow waist)** — no tool schema added. Rung 1: one existing module.

## Failure modes

| Failure | Detected by | Recovery | User sees |
|---|---|---|---|
| Block fence weakens injection defence | I1/I2 hold by `neutralize`, which is unchanged | n/a | nothing |
| Presented count does not rise | `truncated by budget` WARNING's `presented` field | revert one commit | nothing |
| Model stops calling `skill_view` because the suffix moved to the header | `skill_view.execute: entry` rate falls | revert | fewer skills applied |

## Verification

1. `presented` in the truncation WARNING rises from 16; `dropped` falls from 163.
2. `skills_len` on `assemble: exit` changes once at deploy, then holds — Law 1 intact.
3. A neutralization test proves a body containing `</skill_reference>` and a newline cannot
   escape its entry.
4. **The metric that matters:** distinct skills in `skill_view.execute: entry` per week rises
   above the current **8**. This is a *weeks*-long check, not a deploy-day one, and must not
   be claimed early.
